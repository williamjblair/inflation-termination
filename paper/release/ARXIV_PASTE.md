Title:
Inflation for Classical Pair-Source Networks: Termination and Quantitative Obstructions

Authors:
William Blair

Abstract (matches the manuscript):
The classical inflation hierarchy converges to the set of distributions compatible with a correlation scenario, but convergence need not occur at a finite order. We classify finite termination for pair-source scenarios with binary observations: some finite order characterizes compatibility if and only if every connected component of the source graph is a tree of diameter at most three. In that case order two suffices. The classification holds for the Navascués--Wolfe hierarchy and its ancestral-independence and recursively expressible strengthenings; the latter two coincide on pair-source scenarios. The proof reconstructs a model on each double-star and constructs incompatible, strictly positive rational laws accepted at every prescribed order on cycles and the five-observer path. For the triangle and square, the largest total-variation distance of an accepted law from compatibility lies between $c/t$ and $C/\sqrt t$. The upper bound follows from a convex-order argument and depends only on the number of observed outcomes. A separate triangle construction gives a first rejecting order of $\Theta(d^{-1/3})$ along an explicit family at distance $d$ from compatibility, and yields a worst required order of $2^{\Theta(B)}$ for rational inputs of $B$ bits. Cubic moment certificates give explicit rejecting orders for triangle and square laws caught by parity rigidity, including incompatible triangle laws that satisfy every Finner inequality. For scenarios in which a source is shared by three or more observers, we prove termination for a class containing the double-star forests and nontermination whenever a restriction to a pair set is not a double-star forest. Exact certificates accompany the finite computations. The classification for binary observations and finite latent alphabets, together with several triangle results, is formalized in Lean.
