Title:
Inflation for Classical Pair-Source Networks: Termination and Quantitative Obstructions

Authors:
William Blair

Abstract (matches the manuscript):
The classical inflation hierarchy converges to the set of distributions compatible with a correlation scenario. We classify when convergence occurs at a finite order for pair-source scenarios with binary observations: a finite order characterizes compatibility if and only if every connected component of the source graph is a tree of diameter at most three. Order two then suffices. The classification holds for the Navascués--Wolfe hierarchy and its ancestral-independence and recursively expressible strengthenings; the latter two coincide on pair-source scenarios. On every other graph, each prescribed order accepts an incompatible, strictly positive rational law. For the triangle and square, the largest total-variation distance from compatibility among laws accepted at order $t$ lies between $c/t$ and $C/\sqrt t$. Corrected Fourier densities give the lower bound; a convex-order argument gives an upper bound that depends only on the number of observed outcomes. We also prove order-conversion bounds and certify finite-order separations between the hierarchies. Along a separate triangle family at distance $d$ from compatibility, the first rejecting order is $\Theta(d^{-1/3})$. Over rational binary-triangle inputs of at most $B$ bits, the worst required order is $2^{\Theta(B)}$. Exact rational certificates accompany the finite computations. The classification equivalence for binary observations and finite latent alphabets, together with several triangle results, is formalized in Lean.
