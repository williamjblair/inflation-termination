Title:
Inflation for Classical Pair-Source Networks: Termination and Quantitative Obstructions

Authors:
William Blair

Abstract (arXiv field, under the 1920-character limit; the manuscript abstract is longer):
The inflation technique of Wolfe, Spekkens and Fritz tests whether an observed distribution is compatible with a causal structure with latent common causes, and Navascués and Wolfe proved that for correlation scenarios their hierarchy of finite inflation tests is asymptotically complete. They asked whether, for a fixed scenario with fixed observed alphabets, one finite order always suffices, and named the triangle as a possible case of asymptotic-only convergence. We answer the question for every pair-source scenario, in which each latent source is shared by exactly two binary observed variables, so that a scenario is a finite simple graph. A finite order characterizes compatibility if and only if every connected component is a double-star, a tree of diameter at most three; then order two suffices. The same holds for the ancestral-independence and recursively expressible strengthenings of the hierarchy, which coincide on pair-source scenarios. For every other graph and every order $t$ there is a strictly positive rational law that passes the strongest order-$t$ test and is incompatible: a parity witness on every cycle and a witness on the five-observer path refuted by a bilocal inequality. For the triangle and the square, laws accepted at order $t$ can lie at total-variation distance of order $1/t$ from the compatible set, while a convex-order argument bounds the distance by a constant times $t^{-1/2}$. For the triangle we also give an explicit family with a sharp rejecting-order exponent along one direction of approach, an explicit rejecting order under a promised distance, and a $2^{\Theta(B)}$ law for the worst rejecting order over rational inputs of $B$ bits. The triangle nontermination theorem, its fan inequalities, the convex-order bound and the Finner inequality for arbitrary latent spaces are formalized in Lean.
