\section{Order conversion and finite thresholds}\label{app:quantitative}

\subsection{Proof of square order conversion}\label{app:conversion}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:orderconversion}]
The right inclusion is Proposition~\ref{prop:gsound} and the equality is Lemma~\ref{lem:rootsink}. For the left inclusion let $\Gamma$ be an order-$n$ Navascu\'es--Wolfe witness for $P$ with $n=\lfloor3t/2\rfloor$ and let $\Gamma'$ be its restriction to the copy indices $1,\dots,t$ of each source. Restriction preserves symmetry and the diagonal law of degree $t$, so $\Gamma'\in\INW_t$; we check the AI prescriptions.

Fix an AI set of $\Gamma'$ and decompose it into the connected components of its shared-parent graph, which are injectable and pairwise ancestrally independent. A component's ancestry uses two, three or four source types; the two-type components are single copied observations and correspond to edges of the four-cycle $X-Y-Z-W-X$ of source types. Any two subsets of a four-element set of sizes at least three, or of sizes three and two, intersect, so a component using at least three source types conflicts with every other component and needs a diagonal row of its own. Let $b$ be the number of these.

The remaining components form a multigraph on the source-type four-cycle, with multiplicities $n_A,n_B,n_C,n_D$ on the edges $A,B,C,D$. Opposite edges are disjoint, so the colours $\max(n_A,n_C)$ for the pair $\{A,C\}$ and $\max(n_B,n_D)$ for the pair $\{B,D\}$ suffice, and their sum equals the maximum source-type degree $\Delta$: if $n_A\ge n_C$ and $n_B\ge n_D$ the sum is $n_A+n_B=\deg X$, and $\deg X$ dominates the other three degrees. So $b+\Delta$ rows suffice.

Choose a source type of residual degree $\Delta$, say $X$, together with its two neighbours $Y$ and $W$ on the cycle. A component using at least three source types omits at most one, so it uses at least two of $X,Y,W$; each of the $\Delta$ two-type components incident to $X$ uses exactly two of them. The components have pairwise disjoint copied ancestries, and the three families $X,Y,W$ supply $3t$ copies in $\Gamma'$, so $2b+2\Delta\le3t$ and therefore $b+\Delta\le\lfloor3t/2\rfloor=n$.

Assign each component to the diagonal row of its colour. Components sharing a row use disjoint source types, and distinct components use distinct copies within each family, so independent permutations of the copy indices of the four sources place all components on their assigned rows simultaneously. Under $\Gamma$ the rows are independent with law $P$. Within one row the components have disjoint original source types, so Lemma~\ref{lem:sourcedisjoint}, available because $n\ge2$, makes $P$ factor across them. Hence the joint law of the placed components is the product of their $P$-marginals. Undoing the permutations, the AI set of $\Gamma'$ has exactly the prescribed product.
\end{proof}

The factor $3/2$ is the cost of placing all components on diagonal rows at once; ancestrally disjoint collections of $\lfloor3t/2\rfloor$ injectable components whose source-type conflict graph is complete show that this method cannot do better.

\subsection{Count-moment reductions}\label{app:countmoment}

The reductions below replace feasibility for a parity-perfect target by a linear program on the counts of negative auxiliary signs. Let $m\in\{3,4\}$ and use the notation of Section~\ref{sec:cycles}: at order $t$ the copied observation $O_v^{ij}$ reads the source copies $S_{v-1,i}$ and $S_{v,j}$. \emph{Sign potentials} of height $t$ are signs $s=(s_{g,i})$, $g\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}$, $i\in[t]$, one family for each source, and their \emph{potential table} is $O_v^{ij}=s_{v-1,i}s_{v,j}$ as in \eqref{eq:cyclewitness}. The degree tuple $k\in\{0,\dots,t\}^m$ of a set of potential signs counts its members in each family, and $S=\sum_gk_g$. Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)&=\Bigl\{k\in\{0,\dots,t\}^m:\ S\text{ even},\ 2\max_gk_g\le S\Bigr\},\\
D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)&=\bigl\{b_1+\dots+b_t:\ b_r\in\{0,1\}^m\text{ of even weight}\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
A nonzero $b_r$ of even weight has weight at least two, so summing $2\max_gb_{r,g}\le|b_r|$ over $r$ gives $D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)\subseteq D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$.

\begin{lemma}[Sign potentials]\label{lem:potentials}
Let $m\in\{3,4\}$, $t\ge1$ and $q\ge0$ with $P_{m,q}$ a law.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item If an assignment of signs to the copied observations of order $t$ has product $1$ on every copied cycle $\{O_v^{\,i_{v-1}i_v}:0\le v<m\}$, $i\in[t]^m$, then it is the potential table of some $s$, and $s$ is unique up to the global flip $s\mapsto-s$.
\item Let $\rho$ be a law on sign potentials of height $t$ that is invariant under permutations of the indices within each family, and let $D$ be $D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$ or $D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$. Suppose every product of potential signs whose degree tuple lies in $D$ has $\rho$-mean $(-q)^{S/2}$. Then the law of the potential table is a witness placing $P_{m,q}$ in $\IAI_t(C_m)=\Iexp_t(C_m)$ if $D=D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$, and in $\INW_t(C_m)$ if $D=D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Two copied cycles that differ only in the index $i_g$ share every observation except $O_g^{\,i_{g-1}i_g}$ and $O_{g+1}^{\,i_gi_{g+1}}$. Comparing the cycles with $i_g=i$ and $i_g=1$ gives $O_g^{\,ai}O_g^{\,a1}=O_{g+1}^{\,ib}O_{g+1}^{\,1b}$ for all $a,b$, so both sides equal a sign $\lambda_{g,i}$ that depends on neither $a$ nor $b$, and $\lambda_{g,1}=1$. Applying this to the two indices of $O_g$ gives $O_g^{\,ai}=\lambda_{g-1,a}O_g^{\,1i}=\lambda_{g-1,a}\lambda_{g,i}O_g^{\,11}$. Choose signs $c_g$ with $c_{g-1}c_g=O_g^{\,11}$ for every $g$; going around the cycle this is possible exactly because $\prod_gO_g^{\,11}=1$, which is the constraint at $i=(1,\dots,1)$, and the choice is unique up to a global sign. Then $s_{g,i}=c_g\lambda_{g,i}$ are potentials for the assignment. If $s$ and $s'$ are potentials for the same assignment, then $s_{v-1,i}s'_{v-1,i}=s_{v,j}s'_{v,j}$ for all $v,i,j$, so this sign is the same for every family and every index, and $s'=\pm s$.

(ii) A permutation of the copy indices of source $g$ permutes family $g$, so the law of the table is invariant. For a set $F$ of copied observations, $\prod_{O\in F}O$ is the product of the potential signs in $\partial F$, the signs occurring an odd number of times. Let $F_1,\dots,F_r$ be pairwise ancestrally independent injectable sets, $U_i\subseteq F_i$ and $U=\bigcup_iU_i$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary}, $\partial U$ is the disjoint union of the sets $\partial U_i$, each of even size with at most one sign in each family. A nonempty $\partial U_i$ has at least two elements, so it contributes at most $|\partial U_i|/2$ to each $k_g$; hence the degree tuple of $\partial U$ lies in $D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$. If $D=D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$ the hypothesis gives $\E\prod_{O\in U}O=(-q)^{|\partial U|/2}=\prod_i(-q)^{|\partial U_i|/2}$, and by the last claim of Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} and \eqref{eq:cycletarget} the right side is the value of the corresponding character under the product of the $P_{m,q}$-marginals of the blocks. Characters determine a law on a finite sign cube, so every injectable marginal and every ancestral-independence product is as prescribed; the diagonal rows are such blocks, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rootsink} gives the recursive prescriptions. If $D=D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$, the diagonal law is determined by the characters $\prod_r\prod_{v\in F_r}O_v^{\,rr}$ with $F_r\subseteq\{0,\dots,m-1\}$. Distinct rows use distinct indices, so the boundary of such a product has degree tuple $\sum_r\one_{\partial F_r}$, where $\partial F_r$ is the boundary of $F_r$ in $C_m$, an edge set of even size, and the tuple lies in $D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$. Its mean is $\prod_r(-q)^{|\partial F_r|/2}$, the value under $P_{m,q}^{\otimes t}$ by \eqref{eq:cycletarget}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Count-moment reduction]\label{lem:countmoment}
Let $0\le q\le3-2\sqrt2$ and $t\ge1$. Then $P_q\in\IAI_t(\square)$ if and only if there are $w_a\ge0$, $a=(a_X,a_Y,a_Z,a_W)\in\{0,\dots,t\}^4$, with $\sum_aw_a=1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:countmoment}
\sum_aw_a\prod_{g}K^{(t)}_{k_g}(a_g)=(-q)^{S/2}\qquad\text{for every }k\in D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t),
\end{equation}
where
\[
K^{(t)}_k(a)=\binom tk^{-1}\sum_j(-1)^j\binom aj\binom{t-a}{k-j}
\]
is the normalized Krawtchouk polynomial.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The value $K^{(t)}_k(a)$ is the mean of the product of $k$ distinct signs drawn uniformly without replacement from $t$ signs of which $a$ are negative.

Let $\Gamma$ witness $P_q\in\IAI_t(\square)$. Every copied square is injectable, so it has law $P_q$ and product $1$ almost surely, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:potentials}(i) almost every table is a potential table. Choose its potentials with an independent uniform global flip and then apply independent uniform permutations within each family; the law of the table does not change, and the resulting law $\rho$ on potentials is invariant under both operations. Under $\rho$ the mean of a product of potential signs depends only on its degree tuple $k$, and equals $\sum_aw_a\prod_gK^{(t)}_{k_g}(a_g)$, where $w$ is the law of the counts $a_g$ of negative signs. Each $k\in D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$ is the degree tuple of the boundary of an AI set, constructed below, whose prescribed character is $(-q)^{S/2}$; this gives \eqref{eq:countmoment}. Conversely, given $w$, draw $a\sim w$ and place $a_g$ negative signs at uniformly random positions of family $g$, independently across families. The resulting law on potentials is invariant within families, its means are the left sides of \eqref{eq:countmoment}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:potentials}(ii) with $D=D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$ gives the witness.

It remains to realize each $k\in D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$. Regard $k_g$ as the number of terminals in source family $g$, arranged around the source cycle $X-Y-Z-W-X$. A path between two distinct source families gives an injectable block whose boundary consists of its endpoints. We construct paths using at most $t$ copies of each family, with distinct copies assigned to distinct paths. Write $(a,b,c,d)=(k_X,k_Y,k_Z,k_W)$ and, after a cyclic relabelling if necessary, assume $a+c\ge b+d$. Put $r=(a+c-b-d)/2$. The balance condition gives $0\le r\le\min(a,c)$. Pair $r$ terminals of $X$ with $r$ terminals of $Z$. The remaining $X,Z$ terminals number $b+d$ and can be paired with the $Y,W$ terminals along adjacent edges, since each of $X,Z$ is adjacent to each of $Y,W$. Route each of the $r$ opposite pairs through either $Y$ or $W$. There are $2t-b-d$ unused copies in those two families, and
\[
r=\frac{a+c-b-d}{2}\le t-\frac{b+d}{2}\le2t-b-d.
\]
These copies suffice for the internal vertices. Assigning distinct copies at every terminal and internal vertex makes the resulting injectable blocks ancestrally independent, and their union has boundary with degree tuple $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Navascu\'es--Wolfe form]\label{lem:countmomentNW}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:countmoment}, $P_q\in\INW_t(\square)$ if and only if \eqref{eq:countmoment} holds for every $k\in D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$. The same reductions hold for the triangle family $\Pi(-q,-q,-q)=P_{3,q}$, $0\le q\le1/3$, with three families of signs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every copied square is carried to the first diagonal row by independent copy-index permutations, so under a witness it has law $P_q$ and product $1$ almost surely. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:countmoment} then applies with $D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$ in place of $D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$, once every $k\in D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$ is the degree tuple of a prescribed diagonal character. The boundaries of vertex sets of a cycle are exactly its edge sets of even size, so $k=b_1+\dots+b_t$ is the degree tuple of $\prod_r\prod_{v\in F_r}O_v^{\,rr}$ with $\partial F_r=b_r$. For the triangle the same proofs apply with $m=3$. A tuple $(a,b,c)\in D_{\mathrm{AI}}(t)$ is realized by $(a+b-c)/2$, $(b+c-a)/2$ and $(c+a-b)/2$ single-observation blocks between the respective source pairs; these counts are nonnegative integers and use exactly the prescribed number of copies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity along the parity direction]\label{lem:paritymonotone}
Let $H\in\{\mathrm{NW},\mathrm{AI},\mathrm{exp}\}$, $G\in\{\triangle,\square\}$, and $0\le q'\le q$ in the range where the parity target is a law. If the parity target with parameter $q$ lies in $I^H_t(G)$, so does the one with parameter $q'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $q=0$, then $q'=0$ and there is nothing to prove. For $q>0$, Lemmas~\ref{lem:countmoment} and~\ref{lem:countmomentNW} let us take a witness to be a law on sign potentials. Multiply every potential sign by an independent $\pm1$ variable of mean $\rho=\sqrt{q'/q}$. A character whose boundary has $S$ elements is multiplied by $\rho^S$, so its value $(-q)^{S/2}$ becomes $(-q')^{S/2}$, while symmetry and the block structure of every prescription are unchanged. Lemma~\ref{lem:rootsink} covers the recursively expressible test.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:paritythresholds}]
For each order and hierarchy, the artifact contains a nonnegative rational count distribution at the lower endpoint and rational dual functionals whose expectation polynomials are positive on intervals covering $[q_{\mathrm{hi}},q_{\mathrm{cap}}]$. The checker verifies every prescribed moment, dual nonpositivity on all count configurations, and the interval signs by Sturm sequences. Here $q_{\mathrm{cap}}=1/3$ for the triangle and $q_{\mathrm{cap}}=1715728752/10^{10}<3-2\sqrt2$ for the square. For each two-sided bracket, monotonicity (Lemma~\ref{lem:paritymonotone}) extends rejection at $q_{\mathrm{cap}}$ to the remainder of the physical range, and extends the primal witness down to zero. The verification guide linked in Section~\ref{sec:repro} gives the files and commands.

Order one accepts every target law. Proposition~\ref{prop:lowextraction} gives the triangle NW order-two endpoint. The strict comparisons follow by comparing rational endpoints. On the square, the points $3/20$ and $1/10$ lie in the respective gaps; Theorem~\ref{thm:orderconversion} supplies the inclusion $\INW_3\subseteq\IAI_2$.
\end{proof}

Some dual expectation polynomials have simple roots, including $2-\sqrt3$, $1/5$ and $1/7$ in the triangle cases. A root yields an upper obstruction; an exact threshold identity would also require a primal witness at that root.

\subsection{Order conversion along the parity direction}\label{app:parityconversion}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:parityconversion}]
On the square this is Theorem~\ref{thm:orderconversion} for $s\ge2$, since $\INW_t(\square)\subseteq\INW_{\lfloor3s/2\rfloor}(\square)\subseteq\IAI_s(\square)$, and it is trivial for $s=1$. On the triangle, $D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$ is the set of degree sequences of multigraphs with at most $t$ edges on the three source families, since the even-weight vectors of $\{0,1\}^3$ are the zero vector and the three pairs. A tuple $k$ is such a degree sequence exactly when $S=\sum_gk_g$ is even, $2\max_gk_g\le S$ and $S\le2t$. A tuple of $D_{\mathrm{AI}}(s)$ has even $S$ with $2\max_gk_g\le S\le3s$, hence $S\le2\lfloor3s/2\rfloor\le2t$, so $D_{\mathrm{AI}}(s)\subseteq D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$. Now let $\Gamma$ witness $P_{3,q}\in\INW_t(\triangle)$. By the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:countmomentNW}, $\Gamma$ yields a law on sign potentials of height $t$, invariant within each family, under which every product of potential signs with degree tuple in $D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$ has mean $(-q)^{S/2}$. Keep the potential signs with indices in $[s]$. Their law is invariant under permutations of $[s]$ within each family, and every product of them with degree tuple in $D_{\mathrm{AI}}(s)\subseteq D_{\mathrm{NW}}(t)$ has mean $(-q)^{S/2}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:potentials}(ii) with $D=D_{\mathrm{AI}}(s)$ places $P_{3,q}$ in $\IAI_s(\triangle)$.
\end{proof}
