\begin{abstract}
The inflation technique of Wolfe, Spekkens and Fritz tests whether an observed distribution is compatible with a causal structure that has latent common causes. Navascués and Wolfe proved that for correlation scenarios their hierarchy of finite inflation tests is asymptotically complete. They asked whether, for a fixed scenario with fixed observed alphabets, one finite order always suffices, and named the triangle as a possible case of asymptotic-only convergence. We answer the question for every pair-source scenario, in which each latent source is shared by exactly two binary observed variables, so that a scenario is a finite simple graph. A finite order characterizes compatibility if and only if every connected component of the graph is a double-star, a tree of diameter at most three; in that case order two suffices. The same classification holds for the ancestral-independence and recursively expressible strengthenings of the hierarchy, which coincide on every pair-source scenario. For every other graph and every order $t$ there is a strictly positive rational law that passes the strongest order-$t$ test and is incompatible. The obstructions are a parity witness at every order on every cycle and a witness at every order on the five-observer path whose incompatibility is a bilocal inequality; the terminating side is a reconstruction of a model from any order-two witness. For the triangle and the square we measure the failure: laws accepted at order $t$ can lie at total-variation distance of order $1/t$ from the compatible set, while a convex-order argument bounds the distance by a constant times $t^{-1/2}$, so the worst-case convergence exponent lies between $1/2$ and $1$. On the square the two hierarchies differ by at most a factor $3/2$ in the order. For the triangle we also give an explicit nontermination family with a sharp rejecting-order exponent along one direction of approach to the compatible set, the distance asymptotics of that family, an explicit rejecting order under a promised distance, and a $2^{\Theta(B)}$ law for the worst rejecting order over rational inputs of $B$ bits. The triangle nontermination theorem, its fan inequalities, the convex-order bound and the Finner inequality for arbitrary latent spaces are formalized in Lean. The manuscript sources, the exact certificates with their checkers and the Lean development are public at \repo.
\end{abstract}
