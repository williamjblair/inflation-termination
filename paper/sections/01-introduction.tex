\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A correlation scenario has independent latent sources and observed variables that respond to the sources reaching them. The compatible laws of a fixed scenario form a nonconvex set, and finite latent-cardinality bounds give it a finite semialgebraic description, but an explicit characterization is unavailable even for the classical triangle with binary observations \cite{Fritz2012,RGW2018}. Wolfe, Spekkens and Fritz introduced inflation as a test of compatibility \cite{WSF2019}: at order $t$ one copies each source $t$ times and asks for a joint law on the copied observations with prescribed marginals. Navascués and Wolfe proved that their version of the hierarchy is asymptotically complete and asked whether a fixed scenario with fixed observed alphabets admits one finite order that decides compatibility \cite{NW2020}. They named the triangle as a scenario where the answer could be no.

This paper answers the question for every pair-source scenario, meaning every scenario in which each source is shared by exactly two observed variables. Such a scenario is a finite simple graph $G$: vertices are observed variables and edges are sources. The triangle, the square and longer cycles, paths and stars are all pair-source scenarios.

\paragraph{Theorem A: which pair-source scenarios terminate.}
Call a tree of diameter at most three a double-star; it is an edge with any number of leaves at each end, and it includes single edges and stars.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:classification-intro}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph without isolated vertices, with binary observed variables. Some finite order of the Navascués--Wolfe hierarchy characterizes compatibility with $G$ if and only if every connected component of $G$ is a double-star, and then order two suffices. The same holds for the ancestral-independence and the recursively expressible strengthenings of the hierarchy from \cite{WSF2019}, which coincide with each other on every pair-source scenario. When some component is not a double-star, for every $t$ there is a strictly positive rational law that passes every order-$t$ test of all three hierarchies and is not compatible.
\end{theorem}

The terminating half is a reconstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:doublestar}): on a double-star, source-disjoint observed blocks are independent under any order-two witness, and conditioning the witness on a leaf pattern reads off a pair of response tables for the central edge. It applies to arbitrary finite observed alphabets, with the order growing with the largest leaf alphabet. Star scenarios were already known to terminate at order two \cite{NW2020}; the reconstruction shows that the same mechanism reaches diameter three and no further. The obstructing half has two sources. Every graph that is not a double-star forest contains an induced cycle or an induced path on five vertices (Lemma~\ref{lem:exhaustion}), and a witness for an induced subgraph transports to the whole graph (Lemma~\ref{lem:transport}). On every cycle we build a parity witness at every order (Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle}). On the five-vertex path we build a witness at every order whose incompatibility is a bilocal-type inequality across the two inner sources (Theorem~\ref{thm:fivepath}). Both constructions are Fourier densities on auxiliary signs, chosen so that every moment that an inflation test prescribes takes its target value while the density stays positive. The equality of the ancestral-independence and recursively expressible hierarchies is a lemma about inflation graphs in which every latent node is a root and every observed node a sink (Lemma~\ref{lem:rootsink}).

\paragraph{Theorem B: how far accepted laws can be from compatibility.}
For a hierarchy $H$ and a scenario $G$ let $H_t^H(G)$ be the largest total-variation distance from the compatible set among laws accepted at order $t$. Asymptotic completeness says that this tends to zero; the classification says that it is positive at every $t$ off the double-star forests. For the triangle and the square we bracket the rate.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:brackets-intro}
For every $t\ge1$,
\begin{align*}
\frac5{768\,t}&\le H_t^{\mathrm{exp}}(\square)=H_t^{\mathrm{AI}}(\square)\le H_t^{\mathrm{NW}}(\square)\le\min\Bigl\{1,\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2\sqrt t}\Bigr\},\\
\frac{27}{5120\,t}&\le H_t^{\mathrm{exp}}(\triangle)=H_t^{\mathrm{AI}}(\triangle)\le H_t^{\mathrm{NW}}(\triangle)\le\frac{\sqrt7}{2\sqrt t}.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

The lower bounds come from a corrected Fourier density (Lemma~\ref{lem:corrected}) that keeps the parity law at correlation strength $q=\Theta(1/t)$ inside the order-$t$ tests, together with a quantitative rigidity statement for parity-perfect compatible laws. The upper bound is a convex-order argument (Theorem~\ref{thm:convexorder}): under any order-$n$ witness, reading the copied table at independent uniform indices produces a compatible law, and the family of such laws is dominated in convex order by the empirical law of $n$ samples from the target. It gives $H_n^{\mathrm{NW}}(G)\le\sqrt{K-1}/(2\sqrt n)$ for $K$ joint outcomes, without any factor depending on the number of sources. The worst-case exponent on the square and on the triangle therefore lies in $[1/2,1]$; we do not determine it. On the square the two hierarchies are also close in order: $\INW_{\lfloor3t/2\rfloor}(\square)\subseteq\IAI_t(\square)\subseteq\INW_t(\square)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:orderconversion}).

\paragraph{Theorem C: the triangle along an explicit family.}
Before the classification we obtained the triangle case by a different construction, a defect cube, and that construction gives finer information along one direction of approach to the compatible set. For every integer $t\ge1$ put
\[
\eps_t=\frac1{2t^3},\qquad r_t=(1-\eps_t)^{t-1},\qquad
P_t=\eps_t\,\delta_{000}+(1-\eps_t)\,\Bern(r_t)^{\otimes3},
\]
where $\Bern(r)$ takes the value $1$ with probability $r$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main-intro}
For every $t\ge1$, $P_t$ satisfies the complete order-$t$ Navascués--Wolfe inflation test for the triangle, together with every ancestral-independence and recursively expressible prescription, and is not compatible: $P_t(000)^2-P_{t,A}(0)P_{t,B}(0)P_{t,C}(0)\ge\eps_t^2/2>0$, in violation of the Finner inequality.
\end{theorem}

For $0<\eps<1/8$ put $P_\eps=\eps\,\delta_{000}+(1-\eps)\Bern(1-\tfrac12\eps^{2/3})^{\otimes3}$; these laws converge to the compatible point $\delta_{111}$. Their first rejecting order satisfies
\[
\tfrac12\le\liminf_{\eps\downarrow0}\eps^{1/3}\tmin^H(P_\eps)\le\limsup_{\eps\downarrow0}\eps^{1/3}\tmin^H(P_\eps)\le4-2\sqrt3
\]
for each of the three hierarchies (Proposition~\ref{prop:family}). The lower bound is the passing construction; the upper bound is a fan inequality that every order-$t$ feasible law obeys, $tP(000)\le P_A(0)+\binom t2P_B(0)P_C(0)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:fan}), which also gives an explicit rejecting order for any Finner-violating law. Theorem~\ref{thm:distance} shows that $\eps$ is, to leading order, $(1-2^{-3/2})^{-1}$ times the total-variation distance from $P_\eps$ to the compatible set, so along this family the rejecting order grows like the inverse cube root of the distance. This exponent belongs to the family, not to the scenario: Theorem~\ref{thm:brackets-intro} shows that the worst case over all accepted laws decays no faster than $1/t$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:Rp} gives a family approaching compatibility whose rejecting order is $2$. For rational binary-triangle laws with at most $B$ bits the worst required order is $2^{\Theta(B)}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bits}), and a promised distance $\delta$ gives the rejecting-order bound $\lfloor LK/(4\delta^2)\rfloor+1$ in any correlation scenario (Proposition~\ref{prop:promised}).

\paragraph{What is not shown.}
The classification is for pair-source scenarios. Scenarios with a source shared by three or more observed variables are hypergraphs, and neither the reconstruction nor the exhaustion lemma applies to them. The exponent of the worst-case rate is open for every non-double-star graph. The strict separations between the three hierarchies are established at particular orders on the square and are not claimed at every order.

\paragraph{Organization.}
Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the three triangle hierarchies. Section~\ref{sec:pairsource} extends them to pair-source scenarios and proves the root-sink, source-disjointness and transport lemmas. Section~\ref{sec:classification} states Theorem~A and deduces it from Sections~\ref{sec:doublestar} to~\ref{sec:fivepath}, which treat double-stars, cycles together with the quantitative bounds of Theorem~B, and the five-vertex path. Sections~\ref{sec:main} to~\ref{sec:bits} prove Theorem~C and its companions: the defect cube, its incompatibility and survival, the fan inequalities, the exponent, the distance asymptotics, the general distance bound and the bit-length law. Sections~\ref{sec:prior} to~\ref{sec:repro} discuss prior work, open problems and reproducibility. Appendix~\ref{app:supporting} collects supporting results on the triangle and the square that the main theorems do not use.

\paragraph{Method and verification.}
The proofs are analytic. Exact rational certificates check the finite instances: the defect-cube tables at orders one to three, the fan inequality along $P_\eps$, and the new cycle, square, triangle and path witnesses at small orders. Section~\ref{sec:repro} lists the certificates and states which results are formalized in Lean and which are not.
