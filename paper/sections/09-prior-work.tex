\section{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:prior}

\paragraph{Finite-order convergence.}
Navascués and Wolfe \cite[Section 4.1]{NW2020} give a binary family that passes second-order triangle inflation while violating Finner, ask whether any finite order characterizes compatibility, and prove that star scenarios terminate at order two. Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} places the star result inside the double-star class and shows that every other pair-source scenario fails at every order. The terminating half reuses their mechanism, source-disjoint independence at order two, and adds the conditioning step that reads off the central responses; their four-on-line scenario is the first case beyond stars (Corollary~\ref{cor:path}). Henson, Lal and Pusey \cite{HensonLalPusey2014} classify causal structures by whether the observed conditional independences already cut out the classical compatible set; that criterion differs from Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} at the four-on-line scenario, which fails theirs and terminates at order two. Wolfe, Spekkens and Fritz define the recursively expressible sets and leave their general study to future work \cite{WSF2019}; their example of a strict gap between expressible and ancestral-independence prescriptions has an observed node with observed children, outside the root-sink class, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rootsink} shows that no gap exists on that class. Navascués and Wolfe~\cite{NW2020} also exhibit a polynomial optimization problem whose inflation relaxations fail to attain the exact optimum at any finite order, in a scenario where every law is compatible. Our results concern the compatibility tests themselves.

\paragraph{Network inequalities.}
Finner's inequality \cite{Finner1992} supplies the incompatibility test for the defect cube. Renou et al.\ \cite{Renou2019} prove its triangle-network form for quantum sources and for no-signalling boxes combined by local wirings, and give the classical Cauchy--Schwarz argument used here. The incompatibility of the five-path witness is a bilocal inequality across the two inner sources, of the kind introduced by Branciard, Rosset, Gisin and Pironio \cite{BranciardRossetGisinPironio2012}; Lemma~\ref{lem:bilocal} is the classical square-root form with the two endpoint observers treated as settings. The incompatibility of the cycle witnesses is the rigidity of parity-perfect triangle correlations, due to Boreiri et al.\ \cite{BoreiriEtAl2023}, with the noise-robust form of \cite{BoreiriEtAl2025} (Lemma~\ref{lem:quantrigidity} is a moment version that needs no repaired model); Renou and Beigi \cite{RenouBeigi2022PRA} prove token-counting rigidity on every ring scenario. Section~\ref{sec:cycles} reproves the parity statements for arbitrary latent alphabets and stochastic responses.

The first fan inequality follows the marginal-polytope method of Wolfe, Spekkens and Fritz \cite[Section IV.D]{WSF2019}: prove a pointwise inequality on copied observations, then substitute the prescribed marginals. Fraser and Wolfe \cite{FraserWolfe2018} derive triangle inequalities from the wagon-wheel and web inflations. Theorem~\ref{thm:fan} gives a closed formula indexed by the order, together with a second-moment bound. Da Silva, Pozas-Kerstjens and Parisio \cite{daSilvaPozasKerstjensParisio2025} study local models and conjectural boundaries for permutation-invariant binary-triangle distributions; the parity-even face used in Section~\ref{sec:cycles} is one of those symmetric families. Pozas-Kerstjens, Gisin and Renou \cite{PozasKerstjensGisinRenou2023} prove network nonlocality along continuous families of distributions with inflation; the families here are classical and approach the compatible set rather than leave it.

\paragraph{Witness constructions.}
The Fourier densities of Sections~\ref{sec:cycles} and~\ref{sec:fivepath} are signed combinations of characters on auxiliary signs made positive by a real-part or a correction term. Sign-potential representations of parity-perfect arrays and the Krawtchouk reduction of Lemma~\ref{lem:countmoment} are standard in coding theory \cite{MacWilliamsSloane1977}.

\paragraph{Quantitative convergence and effectivity.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:convexorder} strengthens the finite-sampling estimate in \cite{NW2020}: the compatible law extracted from a table is dominated in convex order by an empirical law, which gives $\sqrt{K-1}/(2\sqrt n)$ with no factor depending on the number $L$ of sources, against the factor $L$ in their collision estimate. The other published rates of this family also carry the sources: Gitton's convergence theorem for postselected inflation \cite[Theorem~8]{Gitton2022} has a constant of order $\sqrt{12^{C-1}\sum_c d_cS_c(S_c-\tfrac12)}$ in the source multiplicities $S_c$, and Fraser's combinatorial hierarchy \cite{Fraser2020} converges at rate $O(LC/K)$ in its own parameters. The convex-order domination of sampling without replacement by sampling with replacement goes back to Hoeffding \cite[Theorem~4]{Hoeffding1963}. The bit-length upper bound combines the convex-order estimate with the finite latent-cardinality theorem of Rosset, Gisin and Wolfe \cite{RGW2018}, quantifier elimination, and an elementary bound on nonzero polynomial roots. Its lower bound uses rational members of $P_\eps$.

\paragraph{Quantum inflation.}
Wolfe et al.\ \cite{WolfeEtAl2021QuantumInflation} develop quantum inflation, and Ligthart, Gachechiladze and Gross \cite{LigthartGachechiladzeGross2023} construct a provably convergent variant. The present results apply to classical inflation and the two strengthenings defined in Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:pairsource}.
