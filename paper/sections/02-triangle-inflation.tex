\section{The classical triangle and finite inflation tests}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The triangle}
Three mutually independent latent variables $X,Y,Z$ take values in arbitrary measurable spaces with laws $\mu_X,\mu_Y,\mu_Z$. Three observed variables with finite alphabets are generated by stochastic response kernels,
\[
A\sim\kappa_A(\cdot\mid X,Z),\qquad B\sim\kappa_B(\cdot\mid X,Y),\qquad C\sim\kappa_C(\cdot\mid Z,Y),
\]
conditionally independently given the sources. Write $\Ctri$ for the set of joint laws of $(A,B,C)$ obtained this way for binary alphabets; the notation extends to any fixed finite alphabets. Assign each node an independent random seed and include that seed in one of its incident sources; the other node sharing that source ignores it. This preserves the triangle graph and shows that stochastic and deterministic responses generate the same set of observed laws \cite{WSF2019}; we use whichever description is convenient. By a theorem of Rosset, Gisin and Wolfe \cite{RGW2018}, latent alphabets may be taken finite of bounded size, so $\Ctri$ is compact and semialgebraic; we use compactness in Section~\ref{sec:distance} and semialgebraicity in Section~\ref{sec:bits}.

\subsection{Inflation at order \texorpdfstring{$t$}{t}}
Fix $t\ge1$. The order-$t$ inflation of the triangle has $t$ independent copies $X_1,\dots,X_t$ of $X$, and likewise of $Y$ and $Z$, and the $3t^2$ copied observations
\[
A^{ij}=A(X_i,Z_j),\qquad B^{ik}=B(X_i,Y_k),\qquad C^{jk}=C(Z_j,Y_k),\qquad i,j,k\in[t],
\]
each with the response kernel of its original. The copied ancestors are $\{X_i,Z_j\}$, $\{X_i,Y_k\}$, $\{Z_j,Y_k\}$. A \emph{copied triangle} is $\Delta_{ijk}=\{A^{ij},B^{ik},C^{jk}\}$; the \emph{diagonal} triangles are $\Delta_{\ell\ell\ell}$, $\ell\in[t]$. If $P\in\Ctri$ is realized by some model, then running the inflated model produces a joint law $\Gamma_t$ on all copied observations with the following properties, which are therefore necessary for compatibility: independent permutations of the three index families leave $\Gamma_t$ invariant, every copied triangle has law $P$, and the diagonal triangles, which depend on disjoint sets of source copies, are independent. The finite tests below ask for a joint law with some of these properties.

\begin{definition}[Navascués--Wolfe feasible set]\label{def:NW}
$P\in\INW_t$ if there is a probability law $\Gamma_t$ on $\{0,1\}^{3t^2}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $\Gamma_t$ is invariant under independent permutations of the $X$-, $Z$- and $Y$-copy indices, that is under the group $S_t^3$ acting on the labels $(i,j,k)$; and
\item the diagonal law is a tensor power: $\Law_{\Gamma_t}\bigl((A^{\ell\ell},B^{\ell\ell},C^{\ell\ell})_{\ell=1}^t\bigr)=P^{\otimes t}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This is the full-table symmetric formulation of \cite{NW2020} (equations (16)--(19) in the published numbering; the arXiv version numbers them (17)--(20)), whose degree-$g$ diagonal conditions for $g\le t$ follow from (ii) by marginalization. Navascués and Wolfe index the copies cyclically as $A(U_1^i,U_2^j)$, $B(U_2^i,U_3^j)$, $C(U_3^i,U_1^j)$; our labelling $A^{ij},B^{ik},C^{jk}$ names the same variables with the shared index written in the same position, and the symmetry group is the same. They also note that further relations hold in a genuine inflation, such as $\Law(A^{12},B^{21},C^{12})=P_A\otimes P_B\otimes P_C$ at order two (written here in our indexing), and do not impose them in the hierarchy; those relations are instances of the ancestral-independence prescriptions of Definition~\ref{def:AI} below, which our witness also satisfies. Membership in the Navascués--Wolfe feasible set is a linear feasibility problem in the $2^{3t^2}$ entries of $\Gamma_t$. Navascués and Wolfe prove that $\INW_t\downarrow\Ctri$ as $t\to\infty$, with a quantitative rate recalled in Section~\ref{sec:general}.

\begin{definition}[Injectable sets and the AI feasible set]\label{def:AI}
A set of copied observations is \emph{injectable} if erasing copy indices maps it injectively onto a subset of $\{A,B,C\}$ and the shared latent indices agree; for the triangle these are the subsets of copied triangles. Two sets are \emph{ancestrally independent} if their copied latent ancestors are disjoint. $P\in\IAI_t$ if $P\in\INW_t$ with a witness $\Gamma_t$ under which every injectable set has the corresponding marginal of $P$ and every union of pairwise ancestrally independent injectable sets has the product of the corresponding marginals.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Recursively expressible feasible set]\label{def:exp}
Following \cite[Definition 7]{WSF2019}, expressible sets are generated from injectable sets by two rules: if $X'$ and $Y'$ are $d$-separated by $Z'$ in the inflated causal graph and the laws on $X'\cup Z'$ and $Y'\cup Z'$ are prescribed, then $p(x',y',z')=p(x',z')p(y',z')/p(z')$ (and $0$ when $p(z')=0$) is prescribed on $X'\cup Y'\cup Z'$; and marginals of prescribed sets are prescribed. $P\in\Iexp_t$ if $P\in\IAI_t$ with a witness satisfying every prescription so generated.
\end{definition}

For fixed $P$, each prescription fixes a marginal of $\Gamma_t$, so the strengthened tests are also linear feasibility problems. Restricting a witness to fewer copy indices preserves all three sets of conditions. Thus each hierarchy decreases with $t$, and at a fixed order the feasible sets are nested,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nested}
\Iexp_t\subseteq\IAI_t\subseteq\INW_t,
\end{equation}
and a test is stronger when its feasible set is smaller. All three contain $\Ctri$. We prove our witness lies in the smallest set, so the conclusion holds for all three; the Navascués--Wolfe set is the one whose asymptotic completeness is published and the one named in the question we answer.

For $H\in\{\mathrm{NW},\mathrm{AI},\mathrm{exp}\}$ and $P\notin\Ctri$, write
\[
\tmin^H(P)=\min\{t\ge1:P\notin\mathcal I_t^H\}.
\]
Asymptotic completeness of the NW hierarchy and \eqref{eq:nested} make this a finite integer. We use
\[
d_{\TV}(P,Q)=\tfrac12\sum_x|P(x)-Q(x)|,\qquad
 d_{\TV}(P,\mathcal C)=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal C}d_{\TV}(P,Q),
\]
and write $d_2(P,\mathcal C)=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal C}\|P-Q\|_2$ for Euclidean distance.

\subsection{The Finner inequality}
Every $P\in\Ctri$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finner}
P(000)^2\le P_A(0)\,P_B(0)\,P_C(0),
\end{equation}
where $P_A,P_B,P_C$ are the one-variable marginals. This is the event form of Finner's inequality \cite{Finner1992}, which Renou et al.\ \cite{Renou2019} applied to the triangle network; we include a short proof in Section~\ref{sec:incompat} because we need it for arbitrary stochastic responses and arbitrary latent alphabets.
