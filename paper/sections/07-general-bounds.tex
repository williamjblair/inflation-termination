\section{Rejecting orders from distance, input size and parity}\label{sec:input}

\subsection{Distance-promised rejecting orders}\label{sec:general}

The required order depends on the input law. We give bounds in terms of its distance from compatibility, its rational encoding length and its violation of a cubic parity certificate.

\begin{corollary}[Distance-promised rejecting order]\label{prop:promised}
Let $G$ be a correlation scenario with $K$ joint outcomes, using the private-source convention of Section~\ref{sec:convexorder}, and let $\delta_2=d_2(P,\mathcal C_G)$ and $\delta=d_{\TV}(P,\mathcal C_G)$. If $P\in\INW_n(G)$ then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nw-rate}
\delta_2^2\ \le\ \frac{1-\|P\|_2^2}{n},
\end{equation}
so for incompatible $P$
\[
\tmin^{\mathrm{NW}}(P)\ \le\ \Bigl\lfloor\frac{1-\|P\|_2^2}{\delta_2^2}\Bigr\rfloor+1\ \le\ \Bigl\lfloor\frac{K-1}{4\delta^2}\Bigr\rfloor+1 ;
\]
for the binary triangle, $\tmin^{\mathrm{NW}}(P)\le\lfloor7/(4\delta^2)\rfloor+1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality is \eqref{eq:convexl2}, since some $\omega$ attains at most the mean and $q_\omega$ is compatible. Feasibility at order $n$ then forces $n\le(1-\|P\|_2^2)/\delta_2^2$, and $\|P-Q\|_2^2\ge\|P-Q\|_1^2/K=4d_{\TV}(P,Q)^2/K$ with $\|P\|_2^2\ge1/K$ gives the second form. The triangle has $K=8$.
\end{proof}

The proof uses no convexity of $\mathcal C_G$: we extract one compatible law from the witness. A numerical lower bound on $\delta$ therefore suffices to choose a rejecting order; the result does not supply that lower bound from the input law.

\subsection{Rejecting orders from cubic parity certificates}\label{sec:cubicorder}

\begin{remark}[Cubic certificates and explicit rejecting orders]\label{rem:cubicorder}
In sign notation, every $R\in\Csq$ and every $R'\in\Ctri$ satisfy
\[
\E_RA\,\E_RB\,\E_R[AB]+8\,R(ABCD=-1)\ \ge\ 0,\qquad
\E_{R'}A\,\E_{R'}B\,\E_{R'}C+10\,R'(ABC=-1)\ \ge\ 0 .
\]
Replacing $C$ by $-C$ on the square, and $A$ by $-A$ on the triangle, preserves the compatible sets and gives the companion certificates $\E_RA\,\E_RB\,\E_R[AB]+8\,R(ABCD=1)\ge0$ and $-\E_{R'}A\,\E_{R'}B\,\E_{R'}C+10\,R'(ABC=1)\ge0$.

To prove the certificates, use $|xyz-x'y'z'|\le|x-x'|+|y-y'|+|z-z'|$ on $[-1,1]^3$. On the square, fix $z$ in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:maxmoment}: the moments $\E A$, $\E B$, $\E[AB]$ differ from those of the comparison pair $(A'_z,B'_z)$ by at most $2\Pr(A\ne A'_z)$, $2\Pr(B\ne B'_z)$ and $2e_z$, in total $4e_z\le8\delta_z$, and the comparison product $(\E f_z)^2(\E g_z)^2(\E h_z)^2$ is nonnegative; averaging over $z$ gives the constant $8$. On the triangle, the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:quantrigidity} bounds the error of $\E A$ against $\sigma\tau$ by $1-W$ and the errors of $\E B$ and $\E C$ against $\sigma\nu$ and $\tau\nu$ by $4(1-W)$ together, and $(\sigma\tau\nu)^2\ge0$; the total $5(1-W)$ equals $10\,R'(ABC=-1)$. The certificates add the errors of the parity-rigidity argument~\cite{BoreiriEtAl2023,BoreiriEtAl2025} instead of taking their maximum.

Let $\mathcal F$ be one of these four left-hand sides, read as a function of the law, let $l_1,l_2,l_3$ be the three moments in its product and $\Sigma(P)=3-l_1^2-l_2^2-l_3^2\le3$. If $P\in\INW_n(\square)$, respectively $P\in\INW_n(\triangle)$, then
\[
\mathcal F(P)\ \ge\ -\frac{\Sigma(P)}n,\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\tmin^H(P)\ \le\ \Bigl\lfloor\frac{\Sigma(P)}{-\mathcal F(P)}\Bigr\rfloor+1\ \le\ \Bigl\lfloor\frac3{-\mathcal F(P)}\Bigr\rfloor+1
\]
for $H\in\{\mathrm{NW},\mathrm{AI},\mathrm{exp}\}$ whenever $\mathcal F(P)<0$.
To obtain the order bound, use the compatible laws $q_\omega$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:convexorder}. They satisfy $\E_\Gamma q_\omega=P$. For the three sign functions $\chi_i$ defining the moments $l_i=\E_P\chi_i$, put $h_i=\E_{q_\omega}\chi_i-l_i$. Applying \eqref{eq:convexorder} to $\mu\mapsto(\E_\mu\chi_i-l_i)^2$ gives
\[
\E_\Gamma h_i=0,\qquad \E_\Gamma h_i^2\le\frac{1-l_i^2}{n}.
\]
The product $g(l)=\pm l_1l_2l_3$ has zero diagonal second derivatives and mixed second derivatives of absolute value at most one on $[-1,1]^3$. Taylor's formula along the segment from $l$ to $l+h$ therefore gives
\[
\bigl|g(l+h)-g(l)-\nabla g(l)\cdot h\bigr|
\le\sum_{i<j}|h_ih_j|\le\sum_i h_i^2.
\]
The parity-error term of $\mathcal F$ is linear in the law, so its mean is unchanged. Averaging $\mathcal F(q_\omega)\ge0$ now yields $0\le\E_\Gamma\mathcal F(q_\omega)\le\mathcal F(P)+\Sigma(P)/n$. This proves the bound for NW, and \eqref{eq:nested} transfers it to AI and exp.

Navascués and Wolfe prove that a polynomial inequality valid on the compatible set holds on $\INW_n$ up to an error of order $1/n$~\cite[Theorem 1, eqs.~(34) and (A5)]{NW2020}. Their explicit constant depends on the number of sources; the bound $\Sigma(P)\le3$ here is independent of it. Cubic triangle inequalities of the same form, in $\E A$, $\E B$, $\E C$ and $\E[ABC]$, appear among the order-two inequalities of Wolfe, Spekkens and Fritz~\cite[Section IV C, eqs.~(51)--(54) of arXiv v5]{WSF2019}; the certificates above are not among them. A certificate can reject a law that satisfies the Finner inequality \eqref{eq:finner} at every atom.

For example, the W law, uniform on the three outcomes with exactly one $1$, has $-\E A\,\E B\,\E C+10\,P(ABC=1)=-1/27$ and $\Sigma=8/3$, so the bound gives $\tmin^H\le73$. Its incompatibility with the triangle is Example~2 of~\cite{WSF2019}, and that it satisfies the Finner inequality is noted in~\cite[footnote 4]{Renou2019}. Navascués and Wolfe report that their hierarchy rejects it at order three~\cite[footnote 2]{NW2020}, so the order above is far from tight for this law.
\end{remark}
